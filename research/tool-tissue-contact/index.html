Transferable Tool–Tissue Contact Detection from Stereo Depth in Robot-Assisted Surgery

M. Wu*, Z. Zhang*, H. Yang, A. Kuntz, and J. Y. Wu · *Co-first authors

arXiv

Reliable tool–tissue contact detection can support interaction-aware control and downstream force estimation in robot-assisted surgery. This project uses metric depth reconstructed from stereo endoscopic video to estimate tool–tissue contact.

Motivation

Direct force sensors remain difficult to sterilize, miniaturize, and integrate into existing surgical instruments. Current RGB-based methods can degrade when the phantom geometry, background, or manipulation task changes. This project uses the physical distance between the tool and tissue reconstructed from stereo depth to estimate contact.

Stereo depth reconstruction

Rectified stereo endoscopic frames are processed with FoundationStereo to generate metric depth maps.

Tool segmentation

SAM 2 extracts the tool mask in each frame.

Contact prediction

The depth difference along the segmented tool-mask boundary is provided to a supervised two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model (HMM). The model outputs a binary contact or non-contact prediction for each frame.

Results

The model was trained on six palpation sessions on a cup phantom (8,390 frames) and evaluated on four held-out sessions (2,417 frames). Across tests with unseen phantom geometries and manipulation tasks, it achieved a macro F1 score of 0.93 and an area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC) of 0.98. On the glove session, which introduced both an unseen phantom and an unseen pulling task, it achieved an F1 score of 0.96 and an AUPRC of 0.98.
