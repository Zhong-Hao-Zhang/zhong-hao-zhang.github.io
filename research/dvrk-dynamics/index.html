dVRK-Si Dynamics and Sensorless Force Estimation

Three papers on dVRK-Si dynamics, friction, and sensorless force estimation.

01

Dynamics Estimation for the da Vinci Research Kit: A Review of Model-Based and Learning-Based Approaches

Z. Zhang, H. Zhou, H. Yang, G. S. Fischer, and P. Kazanzides

Machines, 2026

Accurate dynamics are essential for control, simulation, and sensorless force estimation. One longstanding challenge is the accurate modeling of the da Vinci system’s complex dynamics, which is relevant to research in areas such as external force estimation and control. This paper provides a structured narrative review and classification of dynamic modeling methods for the da Vinci Surgical System. We cover model-based and learning-based methods and discuss hybrid approaches as an emerging research direction. We further identify the major technical challenges and promising directions for future research.

02

Friction Compensation for Improved Sensorless Force Estimation in the dVRK-Si

H. Yang, Z. Zhang, L. Al-Zogbi, P. Kazanzides, and J. Y. Wu

In submission

Accurate tool–tissue force information can enable safer manipulation, haptic feedback, and quantitative tissue assessment in robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery, but adding distal force sensors to surgical instruments remains challenging. This paper presents a torque-space compensation and residual-filtering pipeline for improving sensorless force estimation on the dVRK-Si. The method identifies joint friction from quasi-static excitation data, trains a friction-separated free-space torque model, applies friction compensation with velocity-dependent scaling, and then filters the remaining residual with a motion-dependent smooth deadband and exponential moving average.

03

Towards Dynamic Model Identification and Gravity Compensation for the dVRK-Si Patient Side Manipulator

H. Zhou, H. Yang, B. Burkhart, A. Deguet, Z. Zhang, L. Fichera, G. S. Fischer, J. Y. Wu, and P. Kazanzides

IEEE Transactions on Medical Robotics and Bionics (accepted), 2026

The dVRK-Si Patient-Side Manipulator (PSM) mechanism differs substantially from that of the first-generation dVRK and therefore requires a new formulation to accurately capture gravitational effects and improve overall control performance. We first develop a modified DH kinematic model that represents the dVRK-Si PSM’s redesigned mechanism. The equations of motion are then derived using the Euler-Lagrange formulation, and the inverse dynamics are expressed in a linear-in-parameters regressor form. The dynamic parameters are identified from data collected along a periodic excitation trajectory optimized for numerical conditioning and estimated by convex optimization subject to the physical feasibility constraints. The identified model is integrated into the dVRK control stack to enable real-time gravity compensation and computed-torque feedforward control. Experiments on a physical dVRK-Si demonstrate that the gravity compensation reduces steady-state joint errors by up to 84% and decreases end-effector tip drift during static holds from 4.2 mm to 0.7 mm.
